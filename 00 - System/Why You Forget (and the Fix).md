# Why You Forget (and the Fix)

Almost everyone who learns data structures and algorithms runs into the same three walls:
1. **"I see a new problem and can't start."** Without the video, there's no way in.
2. **"I solved it once, and a few weeks later it's gone."**
3. **"I start a problem list and never reach the end,"** let alone the advanced topics.

These are **three separate problems with three separate fixes**. Watching more videos fixes none of them.

---

## Problem 1: "I can't solve a new problem alone"

**Cause:** You're trying to jump straight from the problem to the solution. Good solvers don't do that. They go from the problem, to the **signals** in it, to a **pattern**, then fit the pattern's **template** to the details.

When you watch a video you learn *the solution to that problem*. You don't practice the part that matters in an interview, which is spotting the signal and picking the pattern.

**Fix:**
- Learn patterns **one at a time**, in order ([[Study Plan]]). Inside a pattern block you already know the category, so you only have to work out *how* the pattern applies.
- For every pattern, memorize its **triggers**. See the "Recognize it" section at the top of each pattern note and the [[Pattern Recognition Cheatsheet]].
- Follow the [[Problem-Solving Method]]: a fixed sequence of steps so you never stare at a blank screen.
- Use the **hint ladder**, not the full video. Take the smallest hint that gets you moving again.

## Problem 2: "I solved it but forgot it later"

**Cause:** This is the normal *forgetting curve*. Your brain drops anything you don't retrieve again. Solving a problem once, especially with a video, is recognition ("oh yeah, that makes sense"), not recall ("I can produce this myself").

**Fix:**
- **Active recall**: re-solve problems from a blank file, without looking.
- **Spaced repetition**: re-solve after 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days. Each successful recall makes the memory last longer. See [[Review System]].
- **Compress the lesson.** Every problem note has a one-sentence **Key Insight**. You don't remember 40 lines of code. You remember "sort + fix one + two-pointer the rest," and the code follows from that.
- **Flashcards for triggers** ([[Pattern Flashcards]]), 5 minutes a day.

## Problem 3: "I start and don't finish"

**Cause:** Usually some mix of: too ambitious a daily goal, switching resources, no visible progress, and one missed day turning into a missed month.

**Fix:** [[Commitment Rules]]. In short:
- A **minimum day** so small you can't fail (1 review + 15 minutes).
- **Never miss twice.**
- **One list only** (NeetCode 150) until it's done.
- **Visible progress** (checkboxes in every pattern note, the [[Dashboard]]).

---

> [!important] The mindset shift
> Needing the video isn't failure. It's step 1 of learning. Failure is watching the video and **never re-solving it alone**. From now on, every problem where you needed help gets a re-solve the next day. That rule alone changes everything.
