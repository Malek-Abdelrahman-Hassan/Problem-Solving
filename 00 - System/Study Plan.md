# Study Plan

**Resource:** NeetCode 150 (neetcode.io/practice). Solve on LeetCode, use NeetCode for the roadmap, hints and videos.
**Language:** pick **one** and stay with it. The templates in this vault are Python, which is the shortest to write and what NeetCode uses.

> [!warning] One list. One language. To the end.
> Don't switch to Blind 75, Grind 169, or a new course halfway. Switching resources is the most common way people "restart" forever.

---

## The 3 phases

Go through patterns **in order**. Each pattern builds on the previous ones (Trees need recursion, Graphs need Trees, DP needs Backtracking).

| Phase | Patterns | Problems | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Foundations** | [[Arrays & Hashing]], [[Two Pointers]], [[Sliding Window]], [[Stack]], [[Binary Search]], [[Linked List]] | 45 | Comfortable with the "linear" data structures |
| **2. Recursion & Structures** | [[Trees]], [[Tries]], [[Heap - Priority Queue]], [[Backtracking]], [[Graphs]] | 47 | Recursion becomes natural |
| **3. Optimization** | [[1-D Dynamic Programming]], [[2-D Dynamic Programming]], [[Greedy]], [[Intervals]], [[Advanced Graphs]], [[Math & Geometry]], [[Bit Manipulation]] | 58 | Advanced level |

## How to study one pattern

1. **Read the pattern note** (10 min): triggers, core idea, template.
2. **Type the template from memory** once. Look, close, type, compare.
3. **Solve the Easy problems first.** Easies are where you learn the pattern; Mediums are where you apply it.
4. **Solve the Mediums.** Hards are optional the first time through. Mark them and come back in the second pass.
5. **Finish the pattern:** write 2–3 lines in the pattern note's "My insights" section. What was the trick that kept coming back?

> [!tip] First pass vs. second pass
> **First pass:** all Easy + Medium problems of the NeetCode 150, skipping most Hards (~125 problems). That already gets you to interview-ready.
> **Second pass:** the Hards, plus reviews. This is your "advanced level."

## Weekly rhythm (suggested)

Aim for **5 sessions a week**, 60–90 minutes each. Adjust to your life, but keep the structure:

| Part | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| Review | 10–20 min | Re-solve the problems due today ([[Dashboard]]) |
| New problem | 40–50 min | 1 new problem (2 if they're Easy) using the [[Problem-Solving Method]] |
| Note | 10 min | Fill in the problem note, especially the **Key Insight** |
| Flashcards | 5 min | [[Pattern Flashcards]] |

**One day a week:** a Weekly Review (15 min: new note in `05 - Weekly/`, insert the `Weekly Review` template) plus a **mixed day**: 2 random problems from patterns you've already finished, without knowing the pattern in advance. This trains **pattern recognition**, the skill that solving problems grouped by topic never practices.
